# Active Claim-to-Source Map

This map covers only claims admitted by the current USENIX manuscript. Every listed empirical claim is backed by a source artifact whose fail-fast gate reports `status=passed`.

Claims follow a four-layer evidence order. Source replay establishes committed-effect facts; policy-separating pairs establish authorization relevance; collision tests establish interface conformance on frozen domains; explicit-authority decisions establish that representation changes authorization correctness. Runtime audits separately establish mediation and check--use consistency for one provenance-origin consumer. Evidence from a later layer does not retroactively certify an earlier one.

The controlling claim is authorization observability. A representation induces equivalence classes over executions, and a downstream policy consumer cannot distinguish members of the same class. This claim is representation-independent: typed atoms are one evaluated candidate, not the theory's privileged or universally correct interface. The frozen descriptor is a security-critical consumer contract, not an authority source: it exposes effects to policy but does not define ACLs, delegation, quotas, or user intent.

The interpretation is asymmetric. One source-executed authorization-separating collision falsifies a representation on every domain containing that pair. A zero-collision result establishes only collision freedom for the enumerated domain and policy family; it is not an estimate of open-world generalization. The explicit-authority benchmark uses ACL, capability, and delegation state under a shared engine. Its modules and protocol phases are separated, while authorship remains within one research project.
