# Abstract

LLM agents increasingly call tools that create external side effects, so safety defenses must decide over realized effects, affected resources, authorization context, evidence, and provenance rather than only over tool names or textual surfaces. This draft presents a counterfactual tool-effect invariance audit: decisions should remain stable when the same side effect is expressed through different surfaces, and should change when effect, resource, authorization, evidence, or control provenance crosses the safety boundary.

The evaluated scopes are weak baselines, released TS-Guard and Safiron checkpoints on E47 custom stress, IPIGuard-style topology components, CaMeL-style structural policy components, semantic/evidence diagnostics, and non-deployable oracle or upper-bound rows. The main conclusion is scoped: weak surface baselines fail; stronger checkpoints and structural methods resist some surface shifts; evaluated non-oracle methods remain incomplete for joint effect-resource-authorization-provenance reasoning. Human audit covers 222/222 primary rows and 56/56 secondary rows with agreement 1.0 on decision, effect, resource, and authorization fields. This is a controlled measurement draft, not an original-paper benchmark reproduction or deployed-system safety certification.
